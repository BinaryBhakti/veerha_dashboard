Customers

One screen, and the clearest case of the A1 template earning its keep: the same index machinery as Leads with a different noun and a different question — who has stayed, and who is worth asking back.

7 Customers index /customers A1

Every column the live table carries survives — but four of its six data columns render the same value on all twenty-two rows, so the redesign gives each one something to say: Lifetime spend is right-aligned against its own right-aligned header and states what the money is (a stay, a day visit, or nothing yet), Lists names the list rather than printing an em dash, Since uses one date format for the whole product, and Status separates a lead from a customer who has actually stayed. Selecting rows now shows what selection is for — the live screen has a checkbox on every row and no bulk action anywhere — and the pager carries a rows-per-page control, so twenty-two records stop spilling onto a second page.

7b Customer record /customers/:id state · the vocabulary behind the list

The child route the list opens into, drawn here because it holds the record vocabulary the index inherits: five tabs, and eleven profile fields that read not answered yet on every record in production. The counters stay — but each gap now states its cost, because "0 of 7 filled" is a score, not a reason. Contact actions are the same three everywhere in the product: Email, Call, WhatsApp.
